The power of compound interest for young investors

If you're a young investor, time is your greatest asset. The earlier you start, the longer your money has to compound — and the less you need to put in to end up with a lot.

What is compound interest?

Compound interest is when you earn returns on both your original investment and the returns you've already earned. That creates a snowball effect: the bigger the balance gets, the faster it grows.

Example: $5,000 plus $200 a month at 7%

Start with $5,000, add $200 every month, and assume a 7% annual return compounded monthly:

  • After 10 years: about $44,700
  • After 20 years: about $124,400
  • After 30 years: about $284,600

Wait 10 years before starting and, over the same end date, you'd have about $124,400 instead of $284,600 — less than half as much. Returns aren't guaranteed; this is a simplified illustration.

Why young investors have the biggest advantage

  1. More time = more growth. Even small amounts grow significantly over decades.
  2. Less risk needed. You don't have to chase high-risk investments; time does much of the work.
  3. Small savings can become real wealth. A modest habit today can grow into a large balance later.

How to start investing now

  • Open a high-interest savings account, or check your superannuation is invested to suit your age.
  • Set up automatic monthly investments into index funds, ETFs or shares.
  • Avoid unnecessary debt — compound interest works against you on credit cards and loans.

Final thoughts

Your biggest advantage as a young investor is time. Even starting with $50 or $100 a month lets compounding do the heavy lifting. The key is to start early and stay consistent.
