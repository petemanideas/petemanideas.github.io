Why compound interest is the key to growing your wealth

Compound interest is one of the most powerful financial tools available. Whether you're saving for retirement, investing, or repaying a loan, it can work for you or against you — depending on how you use it.

What is compound interest?

Simple interest is calculated only on your original amount. Compound interest is calculated on the original amount and the interest already added, which creates a snowball effect that speeds up over time.

1. Your money grows faster over time

Invest $10,000 at 5% a year, compounded monthly, and leave it untouched for 20 years: it grows to about $27,126 — more than doubling without adding a cent.

The earlier you start, the bigger the impact of compounding.

2. It helps you save for retirement

Superannuation and investment accounts rely on compounding. Contributing consistently turns small, regular deposits into a substantial retirement balance.

3. Credit card debt uses compound interest against you

The same snowball works in reverse on debt. A $5,000 credit card balance at 20% interest could roughly double in under four years if nothing is paid off.

How to make compound interest work for you

  • Start early: even small amounts grow significantly over time.
  • Look for high-interest savings accounts: accounts that compound daily or monthly maximise returns.
  • Reinvest earnings: leave returns invested so they keep compounding.
  • Pay off debt quickly: avoid getting caught by high-interest loans and credit cards.

Final thoughts

Understanding compound interest is key to financial success. It can be your greatest ally or your biggest enemy — so start early, be consistent, and let your money work for you.
